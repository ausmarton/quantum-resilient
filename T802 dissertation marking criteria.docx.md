

| Item |
| ----- |
| **Abstract** 3%  |
| Concise/brief text only summary of the entire dissertation: aim, objectives, methods, main results and conclusions. Should work as a stand-alone item. Should state what has been done and achieved. Should use appropriate tense (as should the whole of the dissertation). |
|  |
| **Introduction** 10% |
| **Background to the problem / issue** Should explain the problem/issue and its context.  Largely descriptive rather than analytical but should still avoid unsupported assertions and use citations appropriately.  Should not assume specialist knowledge on the part of the reader.  |
| **Justification for the research** This should answer the question, 'why do this research?'.  The potential or actual benefits (e.g. financial, social academic) to key stakeholders should be explained. |
| **Definitions / assumptions/ scope / outline** This is an opportunity to ‘clear the decks’ and get anything that might otherwise remain implicit out into the open. The point is to inform the reader so that nothing concerning the starting points of the dissertation is a surprise later on or is confusing. |
|  |
| **Research definition** 20% |
| **The practical problem / issue** This section should define in some detail the ‘real world’ problem that the research is to address. However the particular issue of interest must be positioned within a broader research context.  A level of abstraction from the specific issue that triggered the study to the generic should be apparent here.  |
| **Existing relevant knowledge** This should contain a critical review of the relevant literature. The range should be appropriate for the aim or purpose of the research. This section might be broken down into appropriate themes.  This section will be judged on the quality of evidence presented (e.g. currency, provenance), its relevance, quantity and range; and on demonstration of skills of critical evaluation of evidence and synthesis.  Overall there should be clear evidence that the primary research is informed by the secondary research. |
| **Aim/objectives/methods/tasks/ deliverables. Also, possibly, research questions/hypothesis** The content and format of this section is likely to depend on the approach taken in terms of the research paradigm. Whatever the approach the purpose of the research should be explicit and clearly stated. Objectives should support the aim and represent the main tasks the research needs to carry out. As such they should be based on active verbs such as ‘identify’ or ‘measure’ and avoid over-generalised statements such as ‘carry out a survey’. Objectives may include activities that require a review of the literature, such as ‘identify relevant theoretical models from a review of the literature’. If objectives are not used then there should be appropriate discussion that links the purpose of the study to the methods used. These need only brief coverage as the next section deals with them explicitly. Tasks and deliverables should be clearly and concisely stated. |
|  |
| **Methodology** 20% |
| **Method\[s\] and techniques chosen** Explains the methods used in the context of the range of methods available. Shows awareness of alternative methods and their respective merits with reference to relevant literature. Makes clear how chosen methods will produce the data required by the answer research questions and therefore achieve research aim. This section is likely to be topic specific in the way it is approached and what is appropriate here needs to have been the subject of discussions between you and your supervisor throughout the research study. |
| **Justification** Provides convincing reasons for choice of method(s).  Demonstrates understanding of limitations of chosen method or interpretation. Justifies any modifications. |
| **Research procedures/ethical issues** Detailed description of method – design and implementation.  Refers to appropriate appendix material e.g. text of questionnaires or interviews. Demonstrates that methods have been applied according to recognised good practice. Ethical issues are identified and any methods to address them described. |
|  |
| **Analysis & interpretation** 20% |
| **Data collection and presentation** Clear and effective presentation of data; e.g. makes use of appropriate summaries, tables, charts and appendices. Appropriate differentiation of raw and derived data.  Explains and justifies any deviation from intended method(s) of data collection as study progresses. |
| **Data analysis** Use and explanation of appropriate techniques (e.g. statistical, graphical, tabular) to analyse data and present results. Evidence of awareness of quality of data and limitations of analysis. |
| **Interpretation of results** This is another section that is likely to vary significantly according to the research paradigm followed. If aim/ objectives have been used to define the research then there should be a clear and logical discussion of the results that shows how and to what extent they achieve the objectives and meet the research aim. If other approaches have been followed then the key aspect is that there should be a clear and logical narrative from the purpose of the study to the outcomes. Within any approach returning to the deliverables as a measure of achievement is appropriate. |
|  |

| Conclusions 10% |
| :---- |
| **Conclusions** Provides a concise summary of the conclusions drawn concerning the purpose of the study. Where this uses objectives and aim, the dissertation template suggests two separate sections.  These will be treated as one for the purpose of allocating marks. Discusses the implications of the conclusions in relation to any limitations that need to be placed on them, for relevant stakeholders.  |
| **Further work** Identifies and briefly explains and justifies further possible research by self or others in the light of the results/conclusions of this dissertation. |
| **Implications of / reflections on the work presented in the dissertation** Significance of results in other situations/contexts. Ethical implications of results. Relevance to other problems or issues. Effectiveness or otherwise of chosen research method. Any significance of carrying out the research for your personal development. Personal reflection on the research and the research process such as what might have been done differently, achievement of personal development objectives. |
|  |
| **Extended Abstract** 7% |
| The extended abstract should summarise the key points in the dissertation and be written at a level appropriate for an audience that knows something about the subject but is not expert. It should include: a summary of the motivation and research aim (or equivalent) a summary of the methodology key results a summary analysis a summary discussion key outcomes. It will typically contain about 2/3 pages of text and some number of pictures /figures /tables /charts in addition to the text. No more than 6 pages in total or 1250 words as counted by word processing software. This word count is in addition to the word count for the dissertation itself. |
|  |
| **Presentation** 10% |
| **Conformity with requirements** Appropriate typeface, margins and spacing. Title page, pages numbered. Citations and references in required format. List of contents, tables and figures with correct page numbers. Within word limits. |
| **Narrative and writing style** Clear coherent storyline developed logically. Acceptable grammar, syntax and spelling. No jargon or unexplained acronyms/abbreviations. No typing or formatting errors. Appropriate tense used throughout. |
| **Structure and organisation** Chapters and sections in appropriate sequence. Figures, graphs and tables appropriately labelled. Lists used appropriately. Citations and references match. Appropriate use of appendices. |
|  |
| The dissertation is marked out of 100\. An overall mark of 50% must be achieved. Each of three thresholds must be met with an individual mark of 50%. Threshold 1 Abstract amalgamated with Extended Abstract, Introduction, and Presentation. (30% of the marks: 15 marks needed to pass) Threshold 2 Research Definition amalgamated with Methodology. (40% of the marks; 20 marks needed to pass) Threshold 3 Analysis & Interpretation amalgamated with Conclusions. (30% of the marks; 15 marks needed to pass) Note that it is possible to gain a mark of 50% or more for the dissertation, but still fail.   This is because each Threshold must be passed individually as indicated above.  If you do not pass one or more Thresholds, this will result in a fail overall.  For example, you may score 25, 20, 10 for each Threshold respectively.  Although this adds up to 55, the dissertation will still fail because the 50% pass mark for Threshold 3 has not been achieved.  Finally, there’s a maximum word count of 15000 words that needs to be adhered to. Anything above 15000 words won’t be marked. This word count applies to Chapters 1 to 5: Introduction, Research Definition, Methodology, Data and Analysis, Conclusion. This word count doesn’t includes any other parts of the dissertation like the abstract, the preliminary pages, references, etc. Additionally, there’s a 250 to 300 word limit on the abstract itself. We need to strictly obey these word counts. |
